What comes to mind when you think of democracy? Do you think of a large building where senators or other politicians gather and make decisions for the country? Do you think of a voter ballot? Or even a group of people raising their hands and voting on an outcome?

While all of these are representations of democracy, the most important part is the people. People who vote, people who debate, people who read and decide for themselves, people who disagree with one another on important topics. At the core of democracy, people make decisions with one another. But what happens to democracy when most people stop?

The consequences of people no longer participating in democracy can be immediately felt by the people living in the country. Public trust in the government erodes, politicians who are the most ideologically extreme get pushed into the spotlight, people become extreme in their beliefs and stop listening to sound reason, regulators stop answering to the public and start answering to the industries that fund their paycheck, and congress gridlocks on issues surrounding cost, raising the cost of living for everyone. Do these symptoms sound familiar?

Voter turnout in the US Elections can offer a revealing snapshot of why these symptoms are appearing. In recent presidential elections, turnout has hovered around 60-66%. Midterm turnout drops to 40-50%, and in 2014 it bottomed out at 36.7% — fewer than four in ten eligible adults. The pattern holds further down. Public comment periods at city councils, school board elections, and midterm elections, these are all seeing less and less turnout as the years progress. The results have a meaningful impact on our lives, but the decisions are made by fewer and fewer people each year.

Then comes the question: why do people not participate? Most of the people who don’t are not bad citizens. They are busy with important matters, tired from working, and told their whole lives that politics is corrupt and their vote doesn’t matter. So they tune out. The problem isn’t the individual: it is what happens to a country when enough people stop caring.

The result? The center-leaning moderates that make up most of the country allow the people with the strongest views to call the shots. Political conversations didn’t disappear, they got more extreme. The loudest, most extreme ideas dominate news headlines, which amplify the division in our country. People stop thinking critically, and start picking teams that make the most sense to them at face value. Political conversations have stopped being debates and started becoming insult matches.

This is the current political world you are walking into.

You are, or will soon become, a legal adult. You’ll be able to vote, write to your representatives, and speak at a city council meeting for things that you believe in. Or you can sit out. But something is for sure: no matter what you decide to do, decisions that influence your day to day life will still be made.

So the question becomes: what are you going to do?
