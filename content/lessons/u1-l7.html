There's no new lesson today. Instead, we're going to look back.

For the last six lessons you've been building something, even if it didn't always feel like it. Not a set of opinions — a set of tools. The unit was called Thinking and Reasoning Foundations, and the foundation is this: you can now approach a political question with the right mental equipment before you ever reach for an answer.

Four tools, specifically. You learned to tell a question about what's true from a question about what's right — fact from value. You learned to hold two real goods in tension and weigh the tradeoff instead of pretending one side has no cost. You learned to turn the audit inward and catch your own bias before you trust it. And you learned to disagree well — to build the other side's case honestly before you answer it, and to deliberate without needing to win.

The point was never to tell you what to think. It was to give you a better way to think — one you'll carry into every unit after this.

So before you write today's Ledger entry, take a minute with the recap on your screen. Each tool is tied back to the lesson where you actually used it. Then the writing prompt asks the real question: what did you take from these four, and where will you use them — in this class, or out in the world where the questions don't come with a background sheet?

If you've got a reflection worth saying out loud, say it. This one's a conversation, not a worksheet.
